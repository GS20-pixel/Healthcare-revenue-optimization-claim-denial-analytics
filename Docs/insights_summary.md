# Business Insights Summary

## Executive Revenue Insights

- Total billed revenue was significantly higher than the collected revenue, indicating revenue leakage during the claim settlement process.
- Revenue leakage highlights the amount that was not successfully collected from insurance claims.

---

## Claim Analysis

- Most insurance claims were successfully approved.
- A smaller percentage of claims were denied or remained pending.
- Improving the denied claims process can increase revenue collection.

---

## Claim Denial Insights

- Certain denial reasons occurred more frequently than others.
- Denied claims contributed directly to revenue leakage.
- Approved appeals represent recoverable revenue opportunities.
- Monitoring monthly denial trends helps identify operational issues over time.

---

## Insurance Provider Insights

- Insurance providers contributed differently to total billed revenue.
- Collection rates varied across insurance providers.
- Average claim values differed between insurance providers.

---

## Patient Demographics

- Female patients slightly outnumbered male patients in the dataset.
- Patients represented a wide range of age groups.
- Demographic analysis helps understand the hospital's patient population.

---

## Revenue Trends

- Monthly billed revenue showed noticeable fluctuations between January–February and April–May.
- Monitoring revenue trends can help identify seasonal or operational changes.

---

## Operational Insights

- Provider workload was distributed across multiple healthcare providers.
- Department-level analysis highlighted differences in patient volume.
- Combining operational and financial metrics provides a better understanding of hospital performance.

---

## Project Outcome

This project demonstrates an end-to-end healthcare analytics workflow by integrating SQL, Python, and Power BI. The analysis identifies revenue leakage, evaluates insurance claim performance, and generates insights that can support data-driven decision-making.
